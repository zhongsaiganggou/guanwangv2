Non-destructive testing (NDT) of steel structures is the inspection of welds and members for flaws without cutting or damaging them. The usual methods are visual testing (VT), ultrasonic testing (UT), magnetic particle testing (MT) and, where the specification requires it, radiographic testing (RT) or liquid penetrant testing (PT). Which method applies, how much welding is tested and what counts as acceptable are set by the project specification, the execution class and the buyer's requirements. ZhongSai performs the agreed scope and issues the reports with the steel.

This guide explains what each method proves, how testing fits the fabrication sequence, and what a buyer should write into the specification before the order is placed.

## What NDT covers on a steel structure

NDT is aimed at the parts of the structure where a hidden flaw would matter: the weld volume, the heat-affected zone and, in thick plate, the base material itself.

- **Safety** — internal weld flaws can grow under load and lead to failure at a load far below the design value.
- **Quality control** — flaws are found in the workshop, where repair is faster and less costly, not on site where it is neither.
- **Evidence** — NDT reports travel with the steel as part of the quality file for the engineer and the authority.
- **Acceptance** — the specification defines the methods, the extent and the criteria; NDT confirms the work against those criteria.

Two neighbouring documents are often confused with NDT. A mill test certificate proves what the steel mill supplied — chemical composition and mechanical properties of a heat number — and says nothing about how the member was welded. A dimensional check proves geometry, not soundness. A project normally needs all three, and none replaces another.

## NDT methods and what each one proves

| Method | What it proves | What it does not cover | Where it is used |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Visual testing (VT)** | Weld profile, throat and leg size, undercut, spatter, surface-breaking cracks, fit-up | Anything below the surface | Every weld, before and after the other methods |
| **Ultrasonic testing (UT)** | Internal planar flaws: lack of fusion, lack of penetration, cracks, lamellar tears in plate | Very complex geometry limits probe access; surfaces must be scanned with couplant | Full-penetration and thick-section welds, plate checks, per spec |
| **Magnetic particle (MT)** | Surface and near-surface cracks in ferromagnetic steel | Austenitic stainless and other non-magnetic grades — use PT instead | Weld toes, heat-affected zone, per spec |
| **Liquid penetrant (PT)** | Surface-breaking cracks in any material, magnetic or not | Sub-surface flaws | Stainless members and joints where MT is unsuitable |
| **Radiographic testing (RT)** | Internal volumetric flaws: porosity, slag, incomplete penetration | Tight planar flaws are unreliable to detect when oriented parallel to the beam; radiation safety controls required | Where the specification calls for RT |

Two practical points decide whether the results are usable: the technician's qualification — certified to a recognised scheme such as ISO 9712 or ASNT Level II, in the method and sector concerned — and the timing. UT and MT are done on bare steel, before surface treatment, because paint and galvanising mask indications and change probe coupling. Testing after coating is a rework decision, not a shortcut.

## Which method for which concern

Buyers usually arrive with a concern rather than a method name. This table maps the common ones.

| Concern on the drawings or in the spec | Method that addresses it | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Surface-breaking crack at a weld toe | VT, then MT (PT on stainless) | MT only works on ferromagnetic steel |
| Lack of fusion on a full-penetration butt weld | UT | RT is weak on tight planar flaws |
| Porosity or slag in a butt weld | RT, or UT | RT leaves a permanent image for the file |
| Lamellar tearing risk in thick plate | UT of the plate before cutting and welding | A material check, not a weld check |
| Weld throat or leg undersize | VT with a weld gauge | Dimensional, not a soundness check |
| Coating thickness or adhesion | Coating test, not weld NDT | Separate test per the paint specification |

If the specification names a method that does not address the actual concern — RT specified for a planar flaw, for example — raise it at the technical review, before fabrication starts.

## When NDT happens in the order

Testing has to be placed in the sequence deliberately, because each method has a window:

1. **Material stage** — plate UT where lamellar tearing or thick sections are a concern, before cutting.
2. **During fabrication** — weld NDT after welding and cooling, before surface preparation.
3. **Final inspection** — visual testing on all welds plus the agreed UT, MT or RT scope.
4. **Pre-shipment** — the buyer or an appointed inspector witnesses the agreed testing and reviews the reports.
5. **On site** — the local team checks visible welds and connections, and reports anything that looks wrong with photos.

The fabrication sequence is described in more detail in [how a steel structure is fabricated](/en/blog/steel-structure-fabrication-process-guide/), and the pre-shipment stage in [pre-shipment inspection](/en/blog/steel-structure-pre-shipment-inspection-guide/).

## What to write into the NDT specification

This is the part that decides whether the reports are accepted later. Nine items, in the order they matter:

- [ ] **Standard and acceptance level** — for example ISO 5817 quality levels B, C or D for welds, AWS D1.1 acceptance criteria, or the governing local code.
- [ ] **Execution class or inspection category** — EN 1090-2 EXC1 to EXC4 where the European frame applies, or the local equivalent; the class drives how much inspection is expected.
- [ ] **Methods per joint type** — butt welds, fillet welds, full versus partial penetration, and any joint the engineer wants covered beyond the default.
- [ ] **Extent** — which members and which welds, and whether coverage is full or partial. The percentage is a specification decision; there is no universal figure, and a supplier quoting one without your spec is guessing.
- [ ] **Hold points and notice** — testing before painting and before packing, with enough notice for a witness to attend.
- [ ] **Personnel** — certification scheme, level and method, with certificates filed with the reports.
- [ ] **Report content** — member and weld identification, method and equipment, operator and level, acceptance criteria, result, date, and the language the reviewer needs.
- [ ] **Repair and retest** — the approved repair procedure, and mandatory retest of the repaired area.
- [ ] **Third party** — who appoints the inspector, who attends, who pays, and what access they get in the workshop.

## How buyers verify the reports

1. Check that every member or weld listed in the inspection plan appears in the report — a report with missing IDs is an incomplete file.
2. Confirm the method and the acceptance criteria on the report match your specification, not the supplier's default.
3. Confirm the operator's certification level and method are recorded and valid for the test date.
4. Cross-check member IDs against the packing list and the erection drawings, so the tested piece is the piece that ships.
5. Note repairs: a repair without a retest record is an open item.
6. File the reports with the [mill test certificates](/en/blog/steel-structure-mill-test-certificate-guide/) so the material and the workmanship evidence sit together.

## Quick answers

### Is NDT the same as a mill test certificate?

No. A mill test certificate certifies the steel the mill produced — heat number, chemistry, mechanical properties, to EN 10204 3.1 or 3.2. NDT inspects the fabricated member and its welds. One proves the material, the other proves the workmanship; both belong in the quality file.

### Who signs the NDT report?

The technician who performed the test signs the record, and the manufacturer's QC counter-signs it as part of the inspection file. Where a third-party inspector is appointed, that inspector issues or endorses the report. The signature carries the certification level of the person who actually did the work.

### What does "acceptable" mean in weld NDT?

Acceptable means the indication falls inside the limits of the agreed standard — for example a defined quality level of ISO 5817, or the acceptance criteria of AWS D1.1, or a project-specific criterion. "Acceptable" without a named standard is not a criterion, and a report that says only "passed" cannot be audited later.

## FAQ

### What NDT is required for structural steelwork?

It depends on the specification and the execution class. The common baseline is visual testing on all welds, plus UT, MT, PT or RT on the joints the engineer nominates. Primary and full-penetration welds usually attract more testing than secondary fillets, but the final scope is the one written in your specification.

### Do all welds need UT or RT?

No. Methods and coverage follow the weld class and the specification. Many projects run visual testing on all welds and add UT, MT or RT on a defined scope of joints, rather than on the whole structure.

### Who performs the NDT?

ZhongSai's QC performs the agreed NDT in the workshop and files the reports; a third-party inspector appointed by the buyer can attend and witness the same testing. The two routes are not exclusive — buyers often use both.

### What reports should I receive?

A usable report lists the members and welds tested, the method and equipment, the operator's qualification level, the acceptance criteria applied, the result of each item, and the date. Certificates for the inspector and the equipment calibration are normally attached.

### What acceptance criteria apply to weld NDT?

The criteria come from the standard named in your specification — ISO 5817 quality levels for weld imperfections, AWS D1.1 for structural welding, or the governing local code — applied to the method used. The report should name the standard and the level, so a reviewer can re-check the result without guessing.

### What if a weld fails NDT?

The indication is recorded, the joint is repaired under an approved repair procedure, and the repaired area is re-tested by the same method before it is accepted. The repair and the retest result both appear in the file, so the history of the joint stays traceable.

### Is NDT included in the price?

NDT is part of the quality scope agreed at order time. Confirm what is included — methods, extent, reports, witness access — and whether third-party inspection is separate, so the comparison between offers is made on the same scope.
