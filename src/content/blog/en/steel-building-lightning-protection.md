Steel buildings generally need lightning protection, but the steel itself can form part of the system: a qualifying metal roof can act as an air terminal, steel columns as down conductors, and foundations or ground electrodes as the earth. The lightning protection level, grounding resistance and surge protective devices are set by a local electrical engineer to the local code.

This guide explains how the steel structure participates, rather than giving fixed values.

![A steel structure building under a distant storm cloud with lightning on the horizon (illustrative concept image)](/images/blog/steel-building-lightning-protection/cover.jpg)

## Is a steel building safe in a lightning storm

A steel frame is not a danger by itself. When the frame is electrically continuous and properly grounded, it can carry lightning current to the earth. The protection level still depends on the building use, contents and local code.

## Parts of a lightning protection system

| Part | Role |
|---|---|
| Air terminal | Intercepts the strike |
| Down conductor | Carries current downward |
| Ground electrode | Disperses current into earth |
| Bonding | Connects metal parts to one potential |
| Surge protective device | Limits surges on cables and equipment |

## Can a metal roof act as an air terminal

A metal roof may be used as an air terminal when it meets the conditions in the local code, commonly involving panel thickness, secure electrical continuity and no combustible layer beneath. These conditions should be confirmed by the local electrical engineer, not assumed.

## Can steel columns act as down conductors

Steel columns can act as down conductors when the joints provide electrical continuity. Connections, coatings and any insulating layers are reviewed so the current path is not broken.

## Grounding and bonding

The earth connection may use foundation steel, driven rods or other electrodes depending on the soil and local code. Bonding connects the frame, metal pipes, cable trays and equipment to one potential. The required grounding resistance is set by the local code, not a universal number.

## What depends on what

The design depends on the lightning protection level, building use and contents, roof and column continuity, soil conditions, and the local electrical code.

## FAQ

### Do steel buildings need lightning protection?

Usually yes, but the level depends on the use, contents and local code.

### Can a metal roof be used as an air terminal?

When it meets the local code conditions for thickness and continuity.

### Can steel columns carry lightning current?

Yes, when the joints give electrical continuity.

### What is the required grounding resistance?

It is set by the local code, so there is no universal number.

### Are surge protective devices needed?

They are specified by the local electrical engineer for cables and equipment.

### Is a steel building unsafe in a storm?

A continuous, grounded frame can carry current safely to earth.

### Does the coating break continuity?

Connections and grounding points are reviewed to keep the path continuous.

### Who designs the grounding?

A local electrical engineer, following the local code and permits.
