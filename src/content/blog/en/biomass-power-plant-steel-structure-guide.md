**Direct answer:** a biomass power plant is not one steel building. It is a set of structures with different loads and different owners: the boiler house frame, the fuel receiving and storage shed, the conveyor galleries and transfer towers, the turbine hall, and small auxiliary buildings. What decides the steel scope is not the fuel type alone, but four interfaces — who supplies the boiler support steel and its load schedule, how fuel dust and moisture set the corrosion class, what crane duty the turbine hall carries, and where the battery limits sit between boiler supplier, EPC and structure fabricator. Get those four wrong and the steel arrives as a pile of parts with no agreed load path.

This guide is written for the buyer, EPC engineer or plant owner who has to split a biomass project into supply packages before asking for a quotation.

## Where steel is used on a biomass power plant

The plant layout decides the steel list. A typical 5–30 MW biomass plant built around a grate or fluidised-bed boiler has these structures, and they rarely belong to the same supplier.

| Structure | What it carries | Who normally controls the design |
|---|---|---|
| Boiler house building frame | Roof, wall cladding, access platforms around the boiler | Structure designer, coordinated to the boiler supplier's envelope |
| Boiler support steel | Boiler pressure parts, ducts, economiser, soot-blowing access | Boiler supplier, or fabricator working to the supplier's load schedule |
| Fuel receiving and storage shed | Long roof spans over the tipping area, walls, dust load | Structure designer, with fuel handling consultant |
| Conveyor galleries and transfer towers | Belt conveyor, material load, walkways, spans between fixed structures | Structure designer, taking conveyor loads from the handling supplier |
| Turbine hall | Maintenance crane, roof, interface with the turbine-generator foundation | Structure designer; crane duty comes from the turbine supplier |
| Auxiliary buildings | Control room, switch room, workshop, weighbridge office, water treatment | Structure designer |
| External pipe racks and duct supports | Steam, air, water and flue duct runs between structures | Piping or process discipline, with structural review |

**What steel structures does a biomass power plant need?** At minimum: a boiler house building, boiler support steel, a fuel receiving shed, conveyor galleries with transfer towers, a turbine hall with a crane runway, and auxiliary buildings. Which of these are supplied together, and which are split between the boiler supplier and the structure fabricator, is a commercial decision made early — and it changes what each quotation actually covers.


## What actually decides the steel design

Six inputs drive the frame, and none of them come from the fuel alone.

- **Boiler loads and where they land.** Boiler support steel takes concentrated loads from pressure parts, plus thermal movement and duct reactions. The load schedule and the interface drawing belong to the boiler supplier; the structure has to be designed around them, not guessed.
- **Fuel type and moisture.** Palm kernel shell, wood chip, rice husk and bagasse behave differently in storage: dust, moisture, condensate and, in some cases, acidic conditions. The fuel yard is usually the most aggressive environment on the plant.
- **Fire strategy.** Separation between fuel handling and the boiler, protected steel near the boiler, and escape routes are fire-engineering decisions that can force a different frame, not just a different paint.
- **Crane duty in the turbine hall.** A maintenance crane runway is a live load with impact; the capacity and hook height come from the turbine supplier, and the runway beam design follows from them.
- **Transport and erection limits.** Module size is limited by container dimensions, flat-rack capacity and what the site can lift. That, in turn, decides how much site welding the design assumes.
- **Local code and permitting.** Wind, seismic, snow and the design standard are set by the project's location and the authority having jurisdiction. They are design inputs, not fabricator preferences.


## The boiler house: a steel frame around someone else's equipment

The boiler house is where most biomass projects lose time. Two arrangements are common, and they are not interchangeable.

| Arrangement | Suits | Advantages | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boiler on its own support structure, inside a simple building shell | Larger boilers; supplier delivers a complete support package | Clear responsibility for boiler loads; building frame stays simple | Two structures in one footprint; access platforms and stairs must be coordinated at the interface |
| Boiler loads carried by the building frame | Smaller plants; tight footprint; supplier without its own support package | Fewer structures, less duplicated steel | Building frame now takes boiler loads and thermal movement; interface risk moves to the structure designer |

**Who designs the steel that supports a boiler?** In most biomass projects the boiler supplier designs the support steel for its own pressure parts and issues the load schedule and interface drawing; the structure fabricator then details and builds to that schedule, or supplies an independent support structure around it. The split varies by supplier and by contract. What must not vary is that the load schedule is issued in writing before fabrication detailing starts.


The fix is a single interface schedule, signed by both suppliers, listing who supplies what at each elevation. It costs one meeting and saves weeks.

## Fuel yard and receiving: where corrosion starts

The fuel yard is a shed with a long span, an open or semi-open front, and a floor that stays damp. It is not a warehouse, and it should not be detailed like one.

Design points that matter more than the span:

- **Roof and wall spans** follow from the tipping bay width and the clearance needed for delivery vehicles, not from a catalogue module.
- **Wind uplift on open sheds** is often underestimated. A shed open on two sides behaves differently from an enclosed building, and the roof fixing detail has to match.
- **Water traps** are the enemy. Ledges that hold dust and condensate, gutters that overflow into the frame, and unsealed roof-to-wall junctions all fail first.

**Why does a fuel yard need a different coating from the boiler house?** Because the exposure is different: fuel dust, moisture, condensate and, in some fuels, acidic conditions, versus a hot, dry boiler house interior. Treating the whole plant to one coating specification usually means over-protecting the boiler house and under-protecting the yard. The corrosion category, coating system and dry-film thickness are set by the owner and the project's environment and required service life; the fabricator applies the specified system and records it, and does not substitute a "standard" system of its own.

## Conveyor galleries and transfer towers

Fuel has to get from the yard to the boiler, and that path is structure: galleries, transfer towers, supports.

- **Loads are not just the belt.** Material load varies with fuel density and fill; wind acts on a long, narrow box; walkways and maintenance access add live load.
- **Support the gallery independently** where possible. Hanging a gallery from the boiler house frame is sometimes unavoidable, but it transfers dust vibration and thermal movement into the building.
- **Head room for belt changes.** A gallery sized to the belt alone will be rebuilt on site when the first belt splice is needed. Monorails, hatch positions and walkway width are structural inputs.
- **Fire separation.** Fuel handling and the boiler are usually separated by fire-rated construction; that requirement changes wall build-up and sometimes the frame layout.

## Turbine hall and auxiliary buildings

The turbine hall is a different problem from the boiler house: a clean, tall, crane-served building over a machine that must not be disturbed.

- **Crane runway.** Capacity and hook height come from the turbine supplier's maintenance requirements. The runway beam, its brackets and the frame that carries them are structural design outputs, and the crane load is a live load with impact.
- **Foundation interface.** The turbine-generator block is normally designed by the turbine supplier and built by local civil contractors. The building frame stands beside it, not on it. The interface — joint detail, access, drainage — has to be drawn.
- **Control room and switch room.** These are light structures with heavy services: cable routes, floor openings, cooling, acoustic separation from the turbine. Deciding them late means cutting holes in finished walls.

## Fire and corrosion: the two assumptions that get missed

Both are usually treated as finish items and both can change the structure.

**Fire.** The fire strategy for a biomass plant is set by the local fire authority together with the insurer, and it decides whether the boiler house frame needs protection, how fuel handling is separated, and where escape routes run. Protection choice — intumescent coating, boards, or concrete encasement — depends on access for inspection and on the required period, and each option changes weights, detailing and programme. A structure detailed before the fire strategy is fixed will be re-detailed.

**Corrosion.** A plant has at least three exposure zones — fuel yard, boiler house interior, and external structures — plus the coastal or inland atmosphere the site sits in. One specification across all four is a false economy in both directions. What the fabricator needs is the specified system per zone, with surface preparation standard, and a record of what was applied.

## Responsibility boundary on an overseas biomass project

Biomass plants are usually built by an EPC with equipment suppliers from several countries, and the structure package sits between them. Splitting the work in writing is the only reliable way to keep a schedule.

| Stage | ZhongSai scope | Local party scope |
|---|---|---|
| Design interface | Engineering coordination, detailing and shop drawings to the agreed loads and interfaces | Registered engineer of record, permits, authority submissions, local code interpretation |
| Civil works | Foundation and anchoring information for the steel scope | Foundations, plinths, turbine block, site civil works |
| Fabrication | Fabrication and QC of the steel scope, including coating to the specified system | — |
| Packing and shipping | Marking, packing, container loading, export coordination and shipping documents | Import clearance, inland transport, port handling |
| Erection | Installation technical guidance and drawing clarification | Local labour, lifting equipment, physical erection, site welding where the design requires it |
| Commissioning support | Structural clarifications during mechanical completion | Plant commissioning, boiler and turbine supplier commissioning |

Written this way, the boundary is checkable. Read as a verbal understanding, it is the first thing disputed when a shipment arrives short.

## What to prepare before asking for a quotation

- [ ] Plant layout showing boiler, fuel yard, conveyor route, turbine hall and auxiliary buildings, with elevations
- [ ] Boiler supplier's load schedule and interface drawing, including thermal movement
- [ ] Conveyor arrangement with belt widths, material loads and support positions
- [ ] Crane capacity, hook height and duty class for the turbine hall
- [ ] Fuel type or mix, moisture range and storage method
- [ ] Local wind, seismic and snow data, plus the design standard to be used
- [ ] Fire strategy or the required fire resistance period per area
- [ ] Corrosion category per zone, specified coating system, or the required service life
- [ ] Transport limits: container or flat-rack sizes, port of discharge, site lifting capacity
- [ ] Battery limits: who supplies platforms, stairs, grating, handrails, monorails and roof drainage

**What does a fabricator need before quoting biomass plant steel?** A layout with elevations, the boiler load schedule, conveyor loads, crane duty, fuel data, local design loads, the fire strategy, the coating specification per zone and the transport limits. Without those, any tonnage figure is an assumption, and any programme is a guess.

## FAQ

**Is the boiler house steel part of the building package or a separate package?**
It depends on the boiler supplier and on the contract, and both arrangements are common. What matters is that the decision is made before detailing and recorded in a single interface schedule.

**Can a fabricator build from the boiler supplier's drawings?**
Yes, provided the supplier issues a load schedule and interface drawing with dimensions and tolerances that the fabricator can detail against. A general arrangement drawing alone is not enough for fabrication.

**Which corrosion protection does a fuel yard need compared with the boiler house?**
The yard usually needs the more protective system, because of fuel dust, moisture and condensate. The boiler house interior is hot and dry. The specific category, system and dry-film thickness are set by the owner and the project exposure, not by the fabricator's default.

**How is a biomass plant structure split for container shipping?**
By transport limit first: panels and modules are sized to container or flat-rack openings, with bolted site joints at positions the erection method can reach. Splitting is a design decision made with the erection plan, not a packing decision made afterwards.

**Do we need a local engineer if the structure is fabricated abroad?**
Yes. Permits, foundations, local code interpretation and authority submissions sit with the registered local engineer. The fabricator's detailing supports that engineer; it does not replace them.

**What causes the most delay on these projects?**
Late interface information — usually the boiler load schedule or the conveyor support positions — followed by a fire strategy fixed after detailing. Both move the structure, not just the drawings.
