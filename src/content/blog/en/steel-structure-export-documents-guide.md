The core steel structure export documents are the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin and customs declaration. The exact files, formats and certifications depend on the destination country, the trade terms, the shipping line and the customs broker. ZhongSai has built steel structures for 20 years and focused on exports for 15, coordinating export documentation for the steel package.

This guide explains the common documents and who prepares them.

![Container terminal with crane and containers (illustrative concept image)](/images/blog/steel-structure-export-documents-guide/cover.jpg)

## Part 1: The common documents in a steel structure export

| Document | Purpose | Prepared by |
|---|---|---|
| Proforma invoice | Confirms the price and terms before the order | Supplier |
| Commercial invoice | Basis for customs value and payment | Supplier |
| Packing list | Shows items, quantities, marks and dimensions per package | Supplier |
| Bill of lading | Title document and receipt of goods | Shipping line |
| Certificate of origin | Claiming origin-based tariff treatment | Supplier or chamber |
| Customs declaration | The actual clearance filing | Customs broker |
| Insurance certificate | Covers the goods in transit when required | Buyer or seller |

The exact set depends on the destination country, trade terms and shipping line — confirm the list with the buyer and the customs broker before shipment.

## Part 2: What the supplier normally provides

The steel supplier normally prepares the commercial invoice, packing list, marks and the supporting factory documents. ZhongSai coordinates the export documentation for the steel package: invoice, packing list, marks and communication with the freight forwarder and customs broker on the files the destination requires.

![Documents and packing list on a desk (illustrative concept image)](/images/blog/steel-structure-export-documents-guide/documents.jpg)

## Part 3: Packing, marks and the packing list

Marks on each package should match the packing list: contract number, package number, destination port and handling marks. The packing list shows the net and gross weights and dimensions per package, so the forwarder can book space and the destination team can plan offloading.

![Container loading of steel members (illustrative concept image)](/images/blog/steel-structure-export-documents-guide/container.jpg)

## Part 4: Check before shipment

- Trade terms and incoterm confirmed with the buyer
- Commercial invoice matches the packing list and the contract
- Marks on the packages match the packing list
- Certificate of origin and destination requirements confirmed
- Shipping details and documents sent to the buyer and broker

## FAQ

### What documents are needed to export steel structures from China?

The common set is commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading and customs declaration; certificate of origin and other certifications depend on the destination country and trade terms.

### Who prepares the export documents?

The supplier prepares the invoice and packing list; the freight forwarder and customs broker handle the shipping and clearance documents.

### Do you provide the certificate of origin?

The requirement depends on the destination country and trade terms; ZhongSai coordinates the export documentation and confirms the files needed with the forwarder and broker.

### What is on the packing list?

Contract number, package number, destination, item description, quantity, and net and gross weights and dimensions per package.

### What does the commercial invoice include?

Description, quantity, unit price, total value, trade terms and the contract reference.

### How are steel members marked for export?

With contract number, package number, destination port and handling marks, matching the packing list.

### Who handles customs clearance at destination?

The buyer's customs broker; the supplier supports with the required documents.

### Do you handle shipping as well?

ZhongSai coordinates the export documentation and container loading; shipping, insurance and destination clearance are agreed with the buyer.
