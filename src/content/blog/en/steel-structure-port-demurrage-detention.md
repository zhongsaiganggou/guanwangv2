Demurrage, detention and port storage charges arise when cargo is not cleared, collected or the container returned within the free time at the destination port. They accrue daily and are usually the buyer's responsibility under the agreed Incoterms, unless caused by the supplier's documents. The most effective way to avoid them is complete documents, early clearance, prompt pickup and fast container return. Free days and rates vary by port and carrier, so confirm them rather than assume fixed values.

This guide explains the three charges, how they are triggered, who usually pays, and how to avoid them.

![Container terminal yard with stacked containers and a gantry crane, representing cargo arriving at the destination port (illustrative concept image)](/images/blog/steel-structure-port-demurrage-detention/cover.jpg)

## The three charges

Exact terms vary by port and carrier, so check the local rules.

- **Demurrage** is charged when a loaded container stays at the terminal beyond the free period before pickup.
- **Detention** is charged when the container is not returned within the free period after pickup, including a late empty return.
- **Port storage** is charged by the terminal or depot for occupying space.

## Free time and billing

There is usually a free period, but its length and the daily rate are set by the carrier, port and contract; they vary by destination and cannot be assumed. Charges accrue per day and per container, and the rate may rise the longer the cargo remains.

## How the charges differ

| Charge | Charged by | Triggered when | Typical payer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demurrage | Carrier/terminal | Loaded box not collected in free time | Buyer |
| Detention | Carrier | Box not returned in free time | Buyer |
| Port storage | Terminal/depot | Cargo occupies space beyond free time | Buyer |

## Why they happen

- Incomplete or incorrect clearance documents
- Duties or fees not paid on time
- Missing import licence, permit or certificate
- Buyer not arranging inland transport
- Customs inspection or port congestion
- No one available to collect the cargo

## Who usually pays

Under common import terms, clearance and collection after arrival are the buyer's responsibility, so these charges normally fall on the buyer. If they are caused by the supplier's late or incorrect documents, the buyer may recover them under the contract. Carrier or port causes, such as congestion, are handled according to the contract and local rules.

## How to avoid the charges

1. Confirm all documents are complete and correct before shipment
2. Arrange any import licence, quota or certificate in advance
3. Contact the clearance agent before the vessel arrives
4. Pay duties and fees promptly
5. Unpack and return the container quickly after pickup
6. State free time and charge responsibility in the contract
7. Track the vessel and arrival dates

## If charges have already started

Collect the cargo as soon as possible to stop the daily accrual, check the invoice against the records, identify the cause to assign responsibility, and keep the documents and correspondence.

## FAQ

### What is demurrage?

A charge when a loaded container is not collected within the free period at the terminal.

### What is detention?

A charge when the container is not returned within the free period after pickup, including a late empty return.

### What is port storage?

A charge by the terminal or depot for occupying space beyond the free period.

### Who usually pays these charges?

Usually the buyer under common import terms, unless the supplier's documents caused the delay.

### How many free days do I get?

There is no fixed number; it depends on the carrier, port and contract.

### How are the charges billed?

They accrue per day and per container, and may increase over time.

### Can I recover charges caused by wrong documents?

If the supplier's documents caused the delay, you may recover them under the contract.

### What should I do once charges start?

Collect the cargo quickly to stop accrual, check the invoice and identify the cause.
