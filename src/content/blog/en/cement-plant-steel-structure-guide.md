**Direct answer:** a cement plant is a group of steel and concrete structures, not one building. Steel normally carries the preheater tower frame, conveyor galleries and transfer towers, coal and additive silos, dust collector and duct supports, the packing and loading area, and part of the clinker storage. What decides the steel scope is the process equipment load schedule, the material surcharge and abrasion inside silos and hoppers, heat radiation around the kiln line, dust accumulation, and where the battery limits sit between the process vendor, the EPC contractor and the steel fabricator.

Most cement plant enquiries arrive as a process package plus a request to quote "the steel building". That framing is the first cost risk, because the steel in a cement plant is mostly support structure for equipment, not a warehouse envelope. A preheater tower is a multi-level frame with platforms and ducts. A clinker silo is a vessel with discharge loads. A conveyor gallery is a line structure between two process units. Each of them is priced from a different drawing set.

This guide sets out which parts are steel, which loads drive the sections, and what a fabricator needs before a quotation is meaningful. The kiln piers and the main mill foundations are usually concrete, and that split is a structural decision, not a supplier preference.

## 1. Which cement plant structures are normally steel

| Area | What the structure carries | Typical steel scope | Main interface |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preheater tower | Multi-level process platforms, cyclone and duct loads, maintenance access | Multi-storey frame, bracing, platform beams, stair and walkway steel | Process vendor load schedule, duct supports |
| Kiln line auxiliaries | Burner platform, tertiary air duct supports, kiln feed and cooler area steel | Support frames, platforms, small buildings | Kiln supplier, civil contractor |
| Raw mill and coal mill area | Mill equipment loads, hot gas ducts, dust collection | Auxiliary frames, duct and filter supports, roof and wall where enclosed | Mill vendor, EPC |
| Clinker silo and cooler | Clinker surcharge, discharge and reclaim loads, abrasion | Steel silo shell and hoppers, or steel support and roof over a concrete silo | Process design, bulk material handling |
| Conveyor galleries and transfer towers | Belt and material loads, walkway and maintenance loads | Galleries, towers, support bents, stair and access steel | Bulk handling vendor |
| Packing plant and loading | Packer, palletizer, truck and bulk loading loads, dust | Frame, platforms, loading canopies, small silos | Packing equipment vendor |
| Additive, coal and fly ash silos | Material surcharge, discharge, aeration, dust | Welded or bolted steel silos with hoppers and supports | Process design |
| Control room, MCC and laboratory | Occupancy and equipment loads, cable trays | Small frames, roof and wall, platforms | EPC, electrical contractor |

The pattern is consistent: steel is used where the structure has to span, support equipment at height, or be built quickly and shipped in pieces. Concrete is used where mass, stiffness or thermal exposure dominates.

## 2. Loads that actually decide the frame

| Load source | Where it appears | What to ask the process vendor |
|---|---|---|
| Equipment dead and live loads | Preheater platforms, mill floors, packer area | Certified load schedule with points, not a total weight |
| Dynamic and vibratory loads | Crushers, screens, mills, fans, packers | Whether the vendor requires isolation, and the allowable vibration at the support |
| Material surcharge and discharge | Silos, hoppers, bins, chutes | Bulk density, angle of repose, discharge method, aeration pressure |
| Abrasion | Clinker and raw meal contact surfaces, chutes, hoppers | Expected wear allowance and replaceable liner strategy |
| Thermal exposure | Kiln shell area, tertiary air and hot gas ducts | Surface temperature range and radiation envelope at the steel |
| Dust accumulation | Roofs, ledges, platform floors, duct supports | Cleaning regime and whether extra roof load allowance is required |
| Maintenance and access | Platforms, monorails, hoist beams, stair towers | Required maintenance loads and access routes |
| Wind on tall slender structures | Preheater tower, silo, stack and duct supports | Local code and design life, not a default wind speed |

Two rules follow. First, a steel fabricator cannot size a preheater frame from an equipment general arrangement drawing alone; the load schedule is the input. Second, silo design starts with material properties, not with a diameter. Both are process data, and both must be frozen before detailing.

**What is the difference between a steel and a concrete clinker silo?** A steel silo is fabricated and shipped in plates or bolted panels, and it can be erected faster on a prepared ring foundation, which suits remote sites and phased construction. A concrete silo is cast in place, has a longer on-site programme, and is normally chosen where local concrete capacity is strong and the silo is very large. In both cases the discharge, aeration and reclaim equipment is the same design problem, and the interface between the silo shell and the extraction system has to be defined before either is priced.

## 3. Steel silo, bolted silo or concrete: which suits which case

| Option | Suits | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Welded steel silo | Medium to large clinker or cement storage where shop welding quality can be controlled | Site welding and coating repair, transport diameter limits, need for a ring foundation |
| Bolted steel silo | Remote sites, fast erection, projects where site welding capacity is weak | More joints to inspect, panel supply and bolt torque discipline, coating at joints |
| Concrete silo with steel roof and support | Very large storage, local concrete capacity available, long project life | Longer programme, slipform or jumpform capability, steel scope reduced to roof, hoppers and access |
| Steel bunker or hopper | Additive, gypsum, fly ash and buffer storage with discharge needs | Abrasion and hang-up behaviour, liner design, dust and aeration |

Kiln piers, mill foundations and large storage are usually concrete because mass and stiffness control vibration and thermal movement. Preheater towers, galleries, duct supports, packing plants and much of the clinker handling steel are fabricated. The dividing line is set by the process vendor's load schedule, the local construction market and the site programme, not by a preference for one material.

## 4. Where the battery limits sit

| Scope item | Usually process vendor | Usually steel fabricator | Must be fixed in contract |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preheater frame design | Process and thermal design, load schedule | Frame, platforms, bracing, connections | Design responsibility for the frame, and who approves the vendor loads |
| Silo shell and hopper | Silo process design, capacity and discharge | Shell, hopper, supports, stiffeners, access | Interface between hopper outlet and extraction equipment |
| Conveyor gallery | Belt, drive and material data | Gallery, towers, bents, walkways | Loads from the belt vendor, and who designs the transfer chutes |
| Duct and filter supports | Duct routing and thermal movement | Support steel, platforms, access | Thermal expansion allowance and tie-in points |
| Packing and loading area | Packer and palletizer layout | Frame, platforms, canopies, small silos | Equipment loads and the loading bay clearance |
| Erection | Process installation sequence | Technical guidance and site support scope | Who controls the erection sequence and crane plan |

**Who is responsible for the preheater tower steel design?** The frame, connections and detailing are normally the steel fabricator's or a structural engineer of record's scope, while the process vendor owns the equipment loads and the thermal design that the frame must carry. The contract has to name who approves the vendor load schedule, because the frame cannot be detailed safely before those loads are fixed.

Most disputes in this plant type are not about tonnage. They are about a support that was assumed to be in the other party's scope, or a load schedule that arrived after steel was detailed.

## 5. Erection and brownfield constraints

1. Survey the access route before detailing: silo diameters, gallery lengths and tower modules are limited by road, port and crane capacity, not by the drawing.
2. Split the structure into modules that can be lifted and landed, not merely transported; a preheater tower module has to be stable when set on its base.
3. Fix the crane plan with the erection contractor early, including tailing capacity and the position of the crane relative to the kiln line.
4. Define hot-work windows if the plant is operating: welding and cutting near the kiln, coal mill and dust collectors need permits and fire watch.
5. Sequence steel with process installation: platforms that carry equipment are often erected first as access, then loaded later.

## 6. What to send for a quotation

- [ ] Plant general arrangement and the battery limit drawing for the steel package
- [ ] Process equipment load schedule, including dynamic loads and support points
- [ ] Silo and hopper data: capacity, bulk density, discharge method, aeration, liner requirements
- [ ] Duct and filter routing with thermal movement allowance
- [ ] Design code and load combinations required by the project, plus the local authority requirement
- [ ] Site conditions: wind and seismic parameters from the project, ambient and dust environment
- [ ] Transport limits: port, road, maximum module size and weight
- [ ] Inspection and documentation requirements: mill certificates, NDT scope, coating system, packing marks

**What drawings do you need to quote steel for a cement plant?** A plant general arrangement with the steel battery limit, the process equipment load schedule, silo and hopper data, duct routing with thermal movement, the design code and load combinations, site wind and seismic parameters, transport limits, and the inspection and documentation requirements. A tonnage enquiry without the load schedule cannot be converted into a firm price.

## 7. Responsibility boundary on an overseas cement project

ZhongSai's scope on an export package normally covers engineering coordination and detailing, fabrication and quality control, marking, packing and container loading, export coordination, and installation technical guidance. The local side normally covers permits and registered engineer approval, foundations and civil works, local labour and physical erection, and authority submissions. Which party designs the preheater frame, and which party signs off the vendor load schedule, must be written down before fabrication starts.

## 8. FAQ

### Does a cement plant need a steel structure at all?
Yes, in specific parts. Preheater towers, conveyor galleries, transfer towers, duct and filter supports, packing plants and additive or coal silos are normally steel. Kiln piers, mill foundations and very large storage are often concrete.

### What steel is needed for a clinker silo?
A shell, hopper, stiffeners, roof, access and a support or ring foundation detail, plus the interface to the discharge and reclaim system. Whether the shell is welded or bolted depends on site welding capacity and transport limits.

### Who designs the steel structure of a cement plant?
It is normally split. The process vendor owns equipment loads and process layouts; the steel fabricator or a structural engineer of record owns the frame, connections and detailing; the local registered engineer handles permits and site compliance.

### What loads must the frame carry near the kiln?
Equipment loads, thermal radiation from the kiln shell and hot ducts, duct and fan weights, maintenance loads and dust accumulation. The design must allow for thermal movement rather than restrain it.

### How is dust handled in the structural design?
Through roof and ledge detailing that avoids accumulation, access for cleaning, closed platform floors where required, and a defined extra load allowance if the operating regime leaves dust on the structure.

### Can steel be erected while the plant is running?
Partly. Brownfield work is sequenced into shutdown windows, with hot-work permits and separation from operating equipment. This is a planning constraint that has to be priced, not assumed away.

### What documents are needed before detailing?
Approved general arrangement, equipment load schedule, silo and hopper data, duct routing, design code, coating and fire requirements, and the interface matrix between process, civil and steel scopes.
