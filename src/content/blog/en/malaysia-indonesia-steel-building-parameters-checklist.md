When erecting a steel building in Malaysia, Indonesia or another Southeast Asian country, the most common problem is usually not fabrication — it is that **the parameters were not clarified before the quotation**. If use, dimensions, loads, crane, cladding or delivery terms are left vague, different suppliers will quote on different assumptions, and omissions, rework and cost overruns follow.

This guide lists the parameters to confirm before contacting a Chinese steel structure manufacturer, organized into six sections. You can use the checklist to prepare your project information before sending drawings or requirements to a supplier.

## 1. Building Use and Basic Dimensions

The building's purpose determines the structural form, and its dimensions drive steel tonnage. Before asking for a quote, clarify:

- **Building type**: production workshop, logistics warehouse, poultry/agricultural building, or multi-story office
- **Span and length**: single or multi-span, each span width, total building length
- **Eave height and clear height**: lowest bottom-chord clearance, whether there is a mezzanine or suspended ceiling
- **Number of floors**: single-story or multi-story, floor load requirements
- **Door and access openings**: main door size, vehicle access method, loading/unloading bay positions
- **Floor slab and equipment layout**: main equipment positions, floor loads, reserved foundation conditions

The structural solution differs significantly by use; workshops and warehouses have different crane, door and ventilation requirements. You can review our [steel workshop](/en/steel-workshop/) and [steel warehouse](/en/steel-warehouse/) solutions first.

## 2. Loads and Applicable Codes

A steel structure must be designed to the loads and codes of the project location; domestic drawings cannot be reused directly. Confirm:

- **Wind load**: local basic wind speed and pressure distribution; wind-resistant detailing should be strengthened in typhoon or monsoon regions
- **Rainfall and drainage**: rainstorm intensity, roof drainage slope and gutter layout
- **Seismic and live loads**: seismic design requirements, roof and floor live loads
- **Hot and humid environment**: corrosion protection, insulation and ventilation for tropical climates
- **Applicable design standard**: Chinese GB, Eurocode/EN 1090, AISC or local code; whether drawings use metric units

### Compliance note for Malaysia projects

Construction works in Malaysia involve **CIDB contractor registration**, and imported steel members may require an approval route such as a Certificate of Approval (COA). These local compliance requirements should be communicated to the supplier at the procurement stage, to avoid incomplete paperwork once members arrive on site. For background, see [CIDB contractor registration and steel building projects](/en/blog/cidb-contractor-registration-steel-building-malaysia/) and [CIDB COA for imported steel structures](/en/blog/cidb-coa-imported-steel-structures/); market context is available for [Malaysia](/en/markets/malaysia/) and [Indonesia](/en/markets/indonesia/).

## 3. Main Materials and Corrosion Protection

Steel grade and anti-corrosion treatment directly affect building service life, especially in the wet, rainy climate of Southeast Asia:

- **Steel grade**: Q235B and Q355B are commonly used, selected by span, load and seismic requirements; the supplier can produce drawings to the target code and provide Mill Test Certificates (MTC)
- **Purlins and framing**: hot-dip galvanized purlins are preferred, such as Z275 galvanized specification, for better corrosion resistance
- **Primer coating**: main members generally use epoxy zinc-rich primer; intermediate and finish coats can be added for highly corrosive environments
- **Connections and contact surfaces**: bolt grade, gusset plate thickness and on-site contact-surface corrosion protection must be specified in the drawings

Do not compare only the price per ton of steel; confirm whether the quoted material grade, coating thickness and MTC are all included. For details on materials and inspection, see [manufacturing and quality control](/en/manufacturing-quality/).

## 4. Crane and Process Equipment

If the workshop will carry a crane or house heavy equipment, these parameters must be frozen before detailing:

- **Crane parameters**: lifting capacity, crane span, rail top elevation, duty class (light/medium/heavy)
- **Crane girder and track**: crane girder section, rail type, braking structure
- **Equipment foundations**: foundations for heavy equipment, anchor bolts, vibration and reserved openings
- **Reserved provisions**: bracket positions, cable festoon systems, maintenance access

If crane information is uncertain, the crane girder and main structure cannot be designed accurately; adding a crane later often requires local reinforcement or rebuilding part of the frame.

## 5. Cladding System and Accessories

Cladding choices affect both operating environment and cost, and should be confirmed item by item:

- **Roof and wall panels**: panel type, thickness, lap and sealing method
- **Insulation**: whether insulation, thermal wool or sandwich panels are required; in hot climates, insulation and ventilation should be prioritized
- **Ventilation and daylighting**: ventilators, skylights, fan layout
- **Doors, windows and drainage**: door types, window positions, gutters and downpipes

Temperatures are high in Southeast Asia; warehouse and workshop insulation and ventilation should be raised at the quotation stage, not added after the main structure is finalized.

## 6. Export Delivery and Commercial Division of Work

For cross-border procurement, the division between fabrication, transport and on-site erection must be clear to avoid unclear responsibilities:

- **Trade terms**: FOB, CIF or other terms; who arranges ocean freight booking
- **Packing and marking**: how members are packed and numbered by unit, for easy on-site counting and lifting
- **Export documents**: commercial invoice, packing list, MTC, certificates of conformity and other accompanying documents
- **Erection division**: local installation crews carry out on-site erection; the supplier provides detailing drawings, member numbering and erection technical guidance
- **Quotation scope**: whether steel, bolts, connection plates, primer and export packing are each included
- **Payment milestones**: division of deposit, progress payments and pre-shipment payment

For the full cross-border delivery process, see [export delivery](/en/export-delivery/); to understand how a quotation is itemized, see the [steel structure quotation guide](/en/steel-structure-quote/).

## FAQ

**Can I ask for a quote without complete drawings?**
Yes. First provide the building use, approximate dimensions, crane requirements and site conditions; the supplier can give a preliminary concept and quotation direction. The formal quote and material take-off are based on shop drawings confirmed by both parties.

**Why should CIDB be raised early in Malaysia projects?**
Local construction involves contractor registration, and imported members may require compliance approval. Communicating these requirements to the supplier and local contractor before ordering prevents incomplete paperwork and schedule delays once the consignment arrives.

**What steel standards do Chinese suppliers use?**
Main members commonly use Q235B and Q355B; drawings can be produced to EN, AISC or other target codes, with Mill Test Certificates supplied with the consignment.

**Who is responsible for on-site erection overseas?**
On-site erection is generally carried out by local installation crews. Zhongsai handles detailing design, factory fabrication, member numbering, export packing and erection technical guidance, and provides remote technical support during installation.
