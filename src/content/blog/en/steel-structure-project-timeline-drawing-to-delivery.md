A typical overseas steel structure project takes **8–16 weeks from confirmed drawings to delivery**, depending on building size, complexity, steel grade availability and shipping schedule. This breakdown covers the main phases so you can plan realistically rather than relying on a single number.

## Typical Project Timeline at a Glance

| Phase | Typical Duration | What Happens |
|-------|-----------------|-------------|
| Drawing review & quotation | 1–2 weeks | Check drawings, loads, code requirements, material specs |
| Engineering detailing | 2–4 weeks | Shop drawings, member sizing, connection design |
| Material procurement | 1–2 weeks | Steel plates, sections, reinforcement order (overlaps with detailing) |
| Fabrication | 3–6 weeks | Cutting, welding, drilling, coating |
| QC & marking | 1 week | Dimensional inspection, surface treatment, component tagging |
| Packing & container loading | 3–5 days | Packing list, photos, container stowage plan |
| Sea freight | 25–45 days | Port-to-port transit (destination dependent) |

The total does not include site preparation, foundation construction or on-site erection — those run in parallel with fabrication and depend on local conditions.

## Phase 1: Drawing Review and Quotation (1–2 Weeks)

The clock does not start until the engineer has enough information to quote accurately.

**What ZhongSai needs at this stage:**

- Architectural or structural drawings (PDF or CAD)
- Building dimensions: length, width, eave height, roof slope
- Load requirements: live load, wind load, snow load (if applicable)
- Crane requirements (if any): capacity, span, hook height
- Cladding requirements: roof/wall panel type, insulation
- Project location and applicable code

If drawings are incomplete or loads are not specified, the quotation process extends. Vague inquiries ("how much for a warehouse?") cannot produce a reliable timeline or price.

**What you get back:** A quotation with member sizes, material quantities, scope of supply and an estimated production schedule.

## Phase 2: Engineering Detailing (2–4 Weeks)

After order confirmation, the project moves to detailed design.

- **Connection design**: Bolt sizes, weld details, bolt patterns
- **Member detailing**: Every beam, column, brace and purlin gets a shop drawing
- **Material takeoff**: Exact tonnage of each steel grade and section
- **Drawing review**: Drawings are sent back to the client or local engineer for approval before fabrication starts

This phase is where schedule delays commonly occur — waiting for drawing approval, clarifying loads, or changing the design after production has started. Clear communication and prompt review at this stage directly shorten the overall timeline.

## Phase 3: Material Procurement (1–2 Weeks, Overlapping)

Once shop drawings are approved, steel plates and sections are ordered from the mill.

- Common grades (Q235B, Q355B / equivalent) are usually available from local stock
- Special grades or non-standard sections may require additional mill lead time
- Material certification (mill test certificates) is provided with each batch

Material procurement typically overlaps with the tail end of detailing — not every member needs full detailing before ordering basic plates.

## Phase 4: Fabrication (3–6 Weeks)

This is the longest phase. Depending on building size and complexity:

- **CNC cutting**: Plasma, flame or saw cutting of plates and sections
- **Assembly**: Jig assembly of beams, columns and trusses
- **Welding**: Submerged arc welding for heavy members, MIG/MAG for secondary components
- **Drilling**: CNC drilling of connection holes for bolted joints
- **Secondary items**: Purlins, bracing, gusset plates, anchor bolt templates

A standard portal frame warehouse (1,000–3,000 m²) typically fabricates in 3–5 weeks. Larger or more complex buildings (mezzanines, crane beams, multi-bay) take longer.

## Phase 5: Quality Control and Marking (1 Week)

Before any component leaves the factory:

- **Dimensional inspection**: Check camber, length, hole positions and straightness
- **Weld inspection**: Visual inspection, magnetic particle or ultrasonic testing as required
- **Surface treatment**: Shot blasting, primer application, touch-up
- **Component marking**: Each piece gets a unique tag (piece mark, project number, bay number) matching the erection drawings

Good component marking is critical for export projects — it tells the local erection crew which beam goes in which bay, without needing to reverse-engineer the structure.

## Phase 6: Packing and Container Loading (3–5 Days)

- Components are grouped by bay and shipping mark
- Long items (columns, rafters) are strapped at multiple points
- Small parts (bolts, clips, sheets) are packed in crates or bundles
- A detailed packing list is prepared with piece marks and container numbers
- Loading photos are taken for each container

The loading plan itself takes a day or two to prepare — it determines how many containers are needed and how weight is distributed.

![Steel structure components being loaded into shipping containers for export](/images/blog/steel-structure-project-timeline/3-container-loading.jpg)

## Phase 7: Sea Freight (25–45 Days)

Port-to-port transit varies by destination:

- Southeast Asia: 7–15 days
- Middle East: 20–30 days
- Africa: 25–40 days
- Europe / East Coast US: 30–45 days

This is outside the manufacturer's control. It depends on shipping line schedules, transshipment routes and port congestion. Add customs clearance time at the destination — typically 3–7 days after vessel arrival.

## What You Can Do to Keep the Project on Schedule

1. **Provide complete drawings upfront** — incomplete drawings extend the quotation and detailing phase
2. **Respond to drawing review quickly** — waiting for approval is the most common delay
3. **Confirm loads and code early** — wind load, snow load, seismic zone and crane requirements should be specified before order
4. **Plan shipping in parallel** — start booking freight once container count is known
5. **Prepare foundations on schedule** — foundations should be ready before components arrive
6. **Don't change design after fabrication starts** — changes mid-production cause rework and delays
